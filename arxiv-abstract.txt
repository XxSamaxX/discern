Recent open implementations read a decision straight from a language model's
answer-token logits instead of decoding text, turning the model into a
runtime-defined conditional - a semantic if. We port this readout unchanged to
a vision-language model and evaluate it on 13,329 items (MMBench en/dev and
POPE) with Qwen3-VL-4B and -2B. The 4B reaches 87.4% on MMBench with one
forward pass per item at 91 ms, and 89.6% on POPE.

Our results concern the confidence signal rather than the score. The
probability these systems report and the logit gap behind it are not competing
signals: the probability is a monotone transform of the gap, exactly so on
binary questions, and both predict correctness equally well (AUC 0.895 against
0.894). What separates them is representational. Past -ln(2^-25) = 17.33 nats
the probability is not representable in float32, the dtype most inference
pipelines carry, and the fraction of decisions crossing that floor grows with
capability: 62% of MMBench items for the 4B against 26% for the 2B, so a better
model loses more of its confidence signal to storage. Within the collapsed set
the gap still separates correct from incorrect at AUC 0.736. Thresholding it at
16 nats answers 66% of items at 98.4% accuracy against an 87.4% base rate.

Repeating the protocol on the smaller model separates what the readout does
from what the model does: AUC for correctness (0.896 against 0.894) and for
order-stability (0.972 against 0.980) replicate, while accuracy, gap magnitude,
flip rate and collapse fraction all move with the model.

Two negative results survive. Difficulty is not epistemics: on 12 normative
questions no model can ground, the gap distribution shifts (AUC 0.788, p =
0.006) but overlaps so heavily that the best threshold still admits 7 of 12.
And our own 22-item probe reported universal softmax saturation and zero order
sensitivity; the 4329-item run refuted both.
